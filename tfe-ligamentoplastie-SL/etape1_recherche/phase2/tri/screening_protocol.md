# Screening protocol: title/abstract stage
Version 1.0, confirmed by Maes on 2026-10-09. Source: PRISMA-P protocol v1.0 (`02_Protocole_PRISMA-P.md`), confirmed by Maes on 2026-10-08.

Review (intervention, PICOS): "Postoperative rehabilitation after surgical scapholunate ligament reconstruction in adults: a systematic review".
Question: in adults who underwent surgical repair or reconstruction of the scapholunate (SL) ligament, which postoperative rehabilitation protocols have been reported since 2020 (content, timing, restrictions, progression criteria), and which functional, clinical and radiological outcomes are associated with them?

## Eligibility (full protocol)
- Population: adults (18 years or older, or at least 80% adults, or adult data reported separately) treated surgically for scapholunate ligament injury, tear, dissociation or instability (acute or chronic, dynamic or static).
- Intervention: any surgical repair or reconstruction of the SL ligament, open or arthroscopic, followed by postoperative care (see definition below). Comparator: another postoperative protocol, or none.
- Outcomes: patient-reported wrist function (primary); pain, range of motion, grip strength, complications, radiological results, return to work or sport (secondary).
- Design: randomised or quasi-randomised trials, non-randomised comparative studies, cohort studies, case series of at least 5 patients.
- Publication date: 2020 or later (already applied in the search).
- Full-text-only criteria: (a) whether the postoperative protocol is described (immobilisation type or duration, or start of motion, plus one more element); (b) the exact number of patients when the abstract does not state it; (c) whether adult, SL-only or non-arthritic data can be separated; (d) the language of the full text. These must NOT be used to exclude at this title/abstract stage. In particular, an abstract that does not mention rehabilitation is NOT a reason to exclude: most surgical studies describe their postoperative protocol only in the full text.

## "Surgical repair or reconstruction of the SL ligament" (interpret broadly at screening)
Counts: SL ligament repair (direct suture, suture anchors, bone tunnels); reconstruction with a tendon graft or tenodesis (Brunelli or modified Brunelli, three-ligament tenodesis or 3LT, SLAM, SLIC or SLICL, ANAFAB, ECRB or ECRL tenodesis, palmaris longus graft); bone-ligament-bone grafts; dorsal or volar capsulodesis or capsuloligamentous repair (Blatt, Berger, Viegas, Szabo, Mathoulin arthroscopic dorsal capsuloligamentous repair); internal brace or suture-tape augmentation; combinations of these; any of these with temporary K-wire or screw fixation.
Does not count on its own: arthroscopic debridement alone; thermal shrinkage alone; reduction-and-association (RASL) or scapholunate screw or K-wire fixation without a named ligament repair, reconstruction or capsulodesis; wrist denervation; salvage procedures (proximal row carpectomy, four-corner or other partial arthrodesis, total arthrodesis, arthroplasty); fixation of fractures without an SL ligament procedure; diagnostic arthroscopy or imaging only.
Exception, treat as unclear: "surgical treatment" or "stabilisation" of SL instability where the procedure is not named; SL ligament repair performed together with fixation of a distal radius fracture (at full text these are excluded unless an SL-specific postoperative protocol and SL-specific results are reported separately).

## Decisions
- "include" (code INC): a clinical study of patients with SL ligament injury or instability treated by surgical repair or reconstruction, reporting patient results or the postoperative protocol. Rehabilitation does NOT need to appear in the abstract.
- "unclear" (code UNC): plausibly eligible but the title/abstract is insufficient, for example:
  (a) the exceptions listed above (procedure not named, SL repair together with distal radius fracture fixation);
  (b) mixed cohorts of carpal ligament surgery (for example SL and lunotriquetral) where SL patients may be reported separately;
  (c) mixed adult and adolescent patients;
  (d) the number of patients is not stated and the record is not obviously a single case;
  (e) no abstract, but the title suggests outcomes of SL ligament surgery.
- "exclude": give ONE code, the first criterion that clearly fails, checked in this order:
  - E1 publication type: review of any kind (narrative, systematic, meta-analysis, scoping), editorial, letter, comment, reply, erratum, conference abstract, study protocol, trial registration, book chapter, surgical technique note without patient outcomes, case report of 1 to 4 patients, survey of surgeons or therapists.
  - E2 not a clinical patient study: cadaver, biomechanical, animal, in vitro, computational or finite-element, imaging studies of healthy wrists.
  - E3 population: not SL ligament injury in adults (for example lunotriquetral or TFCC injury, distal radioulnar joint, scaphoid fracture or nonunion, perilunate dislocation or fracture-dislocation, Kienböck disease, SLAC wrist treated by salvage surgery, children only).
  - E4 intervention: SL injury not treated by surgical repair or reconstruction (conservative or rehabilitation-only treatment, diagnosis or imaging only, debridement or thermal shrinkage alone, RASL or screw or K-wire fixation without a ligament procedure, salvage procedures).
  - E9 other reason (explain).

## Principles
- Sensitive: missing an eligible study is worse than sending an extra one to full text. When genuinely in doubt, choose "unclear".
- Decisive: most records are clearly irrelevant and must be excluded. "unclear" is only for records that plausibly meet BOTH core criteria: SL ligament injury or instability AND surgical repair or reconstruction with patient results.
- Judge content only; records in French, Dutch, German or Spanish, or with an English abstract, are screened normally.
- Use the publication type in each record header to help apply E1, but read the abstract: a "journal article" can still be a review or a technique note.
